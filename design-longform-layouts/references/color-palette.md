# Content-led Color Palette

When the user or reference images do not define a clear palette, derive one rather than choosing colors arbitrarily.

## Workflow

1. Identify the content's core tone, such as rational, warm, serious, energetic, historical, or futuristic.
2. Adjust for context:
   - long-form reading → comfortable sustained contrast and restrained large-area color;
   - poster or feed → stronger immediate recognition;
   - webpage → clear functional hierarchy and states;
   - offline material → environment, substrate, lighting, and print behavior.
3. Check current visual trends and borrow at most one compatible signal, such as neutral temperature, accent hue, saturation, or material treatment.
4. Let content determine direction, context determine contrast strength, and trends only update the feel.
5. Return one recommended palette and one alternative. Briefly explain why each fits, then obtain confirmation before final generation.

Each palette must define:

- background;
- body text;
- surface or module color;
- primary accent;
- secondary accent.

## Long-form constraints

- Keep large background areas at low to medium saturation.
- Maintain clear, stable body-text contrast.
- Reserve high-saturation color for emphasis, numbering, or a visual climax.
- Keep one primary color system across the full image rather than changing the main color by section.
- Do not give a title both the largest type and the strongest color when another element is meant to be the anchor.
- Check color and text at actual phone display scale.

## Avoid

- defaulting technology to blue;
- defaulting history to brown;
- defaulting premium design to black and gold;
- applying a color of the year without a content reason;
- stacking trendy color, gradient, texture, and typography at once;
- sacrificing readability to appear current.
