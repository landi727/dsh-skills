# Collage and Print Illustration

## Analyze

- Identify fragment families and whether they are photographic, drawn, typographic, patterned, or abstract.
- Classify cut, torn, masked, overprinted, misregistered, feathered, and shadowed edges by region.
- Record spatial layer roles, fragment overlap, local reversals, masks/clipping, visibility profiles, scale mismatch, rotation, cropping, and ground exposure. Follow `layering-hidden-shapes.md`.
- Record paper behavior, halftone, ink coverage, trapping, registration offset, grain, wear, and reproduction artifacts only where visible.
- Determine whether apparent volume comes from fragment overlap, cast paper shadow, photographic tonal modeling, drawn value planes, or line-based depth. Record the global mode and fragment-specific overrides through `object-modeling.md`.
- Separate structural print behavior from incidental compression or image noise.
- Determine how repeated fragments, labels, and paper gaps create focal hierarchy and semantic groups.

## Transfer and prompt

Define a limited fragment system, edge taxonomy, partial layer stack, overlap and interlock rules, fragment visibility priorities, object-modeling profile, print-color relationship, and ground material. Assign fragments to semantic groups and density clusters. Rebuild target content from new fragments while preserving the assembly grammar.

State the scale and location of print artifacts, fragment-level tonal modeling, and cast-shadow ceiling. Avoid global paper noise, random torn edges, or added three-dimensional material effects when the reference uses clean flat fragments.

## Accept

Check fragment-family consistency, edge types, spatial and production order, overlap logic, local reversals, masks, protected fragment visibility, modeling mode by fragment type, cast-shadow footprint, detail density, scale contrast, ground exposure, registration behavior, and artifact placement. Reject collage effects that read as an undirected texture overlay or unsupported three-dimensional assembly.
