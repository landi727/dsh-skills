# Visual Organization

## Required structure

Inspect the five systems below independently. Expand findings that affect this reference or target; keep simple systems brief. For every decisive finding, retain:

- observed organization and evidence;
- scope and confidence;
- reusable rule;
- target-dependent variables;
- generation constraint;
- visible failure sign;
- acceptance check.

Keep these systems distinct from density rhythm, object inventory, physical depth, and production-layer order.
Map each semantic or focal role to a layer role only after analyzing the systems independently; frontmost does not automatically mean first-read or most important.

## 1. Focal hierarchy and viewing path

Record the first-read entry point, primary focal point, secondary focal points, ordered viewing path, loops or exits, and the devices producing salience. Check size, contrast, saturation, isolation, face or gaze direction, directional lines, framing, and text hierarchy. State how the path survives at thumbnail size.

For transfer, preserve the hierarchy and path relation while assigning new target content to the same roles. Do not retain source nouns merely to reproduce the path.

## 2. Visual weight

Record how area, value contrast, saturation, edge density, detail, position, isolation, and frame contact produce weight. Identify the balance axis, dominant mass, counterweights, edge tension, and stable or intentionally unstable equilibrium.

For transfer, preserve weight relationships rather than copying coordinates. A small high-contrast label may counterbalance a large quiet form.

## 3. Motif grammar

Identify repeated shape, line, border, color, symbol, or directional families. Record motif frequency, spacing, scale progression, orientation, alternation, clustering, and controlled mutation. Separate motif repetition from random texture and one-off decoration.

For transfer, define how new content is translated into the motif family and which variations remain allowed. Do not copy literal source symbols unless frozen.

## 4. Graphic narrative devices

Identify windows, panels, cutaways, frames, arrows, trails, labels, diagrams, sequences, badges, captions, and nested scenes. Record the functional role of each device: introduce, explain, connect, compare, reveal, sequence, annotate, or conclude.

For transfer, preserve device roles and interactions. Recompute their content, shape, count, and placement from the target story and density budget.

## 5. Semantic grouping

Group elements by story, function, topic, or visual module. Record group boundaries, dominant and supporting groups, membership, internal hierarchy, and the connectors between groups. Separate semantic grouping from simple proximity when color, frames, arrows, or repetition create the group.

For transfer, assign every included target element to a group and role. Preserve required recognition and story content; consolidate or omit optional elements with no useful role. Preserve the reference's group hierarchy and connection grammar without importing source content.

## Prompt and acceptance

Follow generation-blueprint-schema.md for the final prompt order. Put decisive focal, action, weight, motif, or grouping relationships in the ranked visual core. Place remaining organization controls near the target groups they govern. Color, edge, brushwork, or other mechanisms may precede organization when they carry more of the reference's identity.

At acceptance, inspect the full frame and a thumbnail; add mild blur when it clarifies a consequential mass or density claim. Confirm source-specific viewing order, balance, grouping, narrative function, and cadence before incidental decorative accuracy. Do not impose a universal focal path on an intentionally diffuse composition.
