# Line, Color, Fill, Material, Volume, and Texture

## Contents

1. Color, fill, material, light, and volume
2. Contours, borders, and internal lines
3. Multi-region line checks
4. Texture and detail distribution

## 1. Color construction

Follow `color-relationships.md` for the executable role map, palette mode, hue-distance structure, temperature allocation, value ladder, adjacency matrix, and color-adhesion audit. This section records supporting chromatic and surface observations.

Conclude:

- saturation range and concentration;
- lightness distribution;
- overall value span;
- darkest and lightest functions;
- hue breadth;
- warm/cool organization;
- area dominance versus accent concentration;
- opacity and transparency;
- separation through hue, value, temperature, outline, neutral gap, or texture.

Do not use “colorful,” “bold,” or “accent color” as the complete conclusion.

## 2. Fill mechanism

Analyze separately from color:

- flat or gradient;
- opaque or translucent;
- dense or thin;
- even or brush-marked;
- clean, bleeding, offset, broken, or boundary-crossing;
- interior variation;
- relationship to outlines.

## 3. Material, light, and volume

Separate visible material evidence from medium or process evocation.
Follow `object-modeling.md` for the structured modeling mode, value-plane system, lighting ceiling, material-response rules, line-based volume, detail-density profile, and object-tier limits. Keep this section as supporting surface evidence.

Describe:

- local color versus illumination;
- highlight, mid-plane, dark plane, shadow, and cast shadow;
- discrete faceted planes versus continuous tonal modeling;
- visible side thickness;
- whether light is physically coherent or graphic;
- how volume remains legible without gradients.

Do not let a real-world material label authorize highlights, reflections, refraction, ambient occlusion, gradients, or extra geometry that the source does not visibly use.

## 4. Line system

Treat these as independent systems.

### Subject and silhouette contours

Conclude:

- color and value;
- dominant thickness and variation;
- continuity and closure;
- smoothness, wobble, pressure, breaks, and open contours;
- corner behavior;
- relationship to fills;
- contribution to recognition and volume;
- meaningful exceptions.

### External frames and borders

Conclude:

- single, double, or multiple layers;
- outer/inner line hierarchy;
- spacing and parallelism;
- corner construction;
- color relationships;
- whether borders imply panel thickness, framing, registration, or decoration;
- variation across object types.

### Internal lines

Conclude:

- structural seams, divisions, folds, hatching, symbols, lettering, or decoration;
- thickness relative to subject contours;
- density and continuity;
- whether lines describe structure, volume, movement, material, or ornament;
- hierarchy among different functions.

## 5. Multi-region line rule

Before issuing a global or dominant line conclusion:

- inspect separated regions;
- compare different object types when available;
- compare top, middle, and bottom or equivalent spatial zones;
- seek at least one possible counterexample;
- classify the result as global, dominant, local, or mixed.

Do not expose this sampling process by default. Output the corrected conclusion and decisive exception only.

## 6. Texture summary

Keep the final texture section proportionate to its role. When texture is decisive, specify mark scale relative to object/canvas, density, direction, contrast, edge distribution, and interaction with color and shape; separate these stable behaviors from individual random marks. Otherwise summarize briefly:

- **overall texture:** flat, grainy, fibrous, mottled, scratched, brush-marked, digitally uniform, or mixed;
- **distinctive local texture:** only the regions whose texture materially differs from the overall field.

Determine internally:

- whether texture crosses object boundaries;
- whether it follows a fill;
- whether different colors behave differently;
- whether apparent noise may be compression.

Do not turn one speckled or dry-brushed area into a global texture claim.

## 7. Detail distribution

Retain detailed object analysis, but summarize global distribution through:

- focal density;
- ordinary-object density;
- quiet zones;
- text, hardware, decoration, material, and narrative detail;
- whether color interiors remain simple while attachments remain complex.

Route object-internal density, protected clean masses, and tier progression to `object-modeling.md`; route full-canvas quiet and dense zones to `density-rhythm.md`.
