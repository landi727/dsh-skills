# Painterly Illustration

## Analyze

- Record mark scale relative to object and canvas, stroke direction, length, curvature, pressure, repetition, and overlap.
- Map hard, soft, lost, found, broken, and fill-defined edges by focal importance.
- Record opacity, glazing, scumbling, wash, impasto evocation, gaps, bleeding, color mixing, and underlayer visibility only where observed.
- Distinguish spatial occlusion from visible paint-layer overlap, lost edges, transparent passages, and later edge restatement. Follow `layering-hidden-shapes.md`.
- Separate large value masses from local hue variation and surface marks.
- Record the modeling mode, value span, light/shadow mass relation, material-response range, and object-tier detail progression. Follow `object-modeling.md`; do not equate visible brushwork with strong volume.
- Determine whether strokes describe form, light, movement, material, atmosphere, or decoration.
- Distinguish visible process evidence from a digital simulation or medium label.

## Transfer and prompt

Preserve mark behavior by function and region. Specify which edges remain controlled, where strokes follow form, where colors mix, how underlayers remain visible, which edges are restated above overlaps, and how focal areas receive higher definition. Scale marks to the target object and canvas instead of copying individual strokes.

Follow generation-blueprint-schema.md for prompt order. Prioritize the source's decisive mass, edge, mark, or color behavior; a medium label does not replace those mechanisms. Preserve clean/weakly modeled regions when present and dense/strongly modeled passages when decisive. Avoid applying one uniform brush, grain filter, glossy response, or continuous modeling strength across all regions.

## Accept

Check large value organization, spatial and paint-layer order, underlayer visibility, edge restatement, modeling mode, light/shadow footprint, material-response range, edge hierarchy, mark scale and direction, opacity behavior, color mixing, clean-area preservation, focal-detail concentration, and object-tier exceptions. Treat uniform texture or uniform modeling strength across shapes as a failure of painterly transfer.
