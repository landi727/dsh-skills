# Validation and Repair

Validate observable relationships rather than prompt wording.

## Inspection views

Inspect:

- full frame at normal size;
- thumbnail scale;
- mildly blurred view when density or first-read order is uncertain;
- local crops of every inventoried important subject and every inset scene;
- a wider contextual crop for important environment roles whose identity depends on spatial relations.

Before inspection, create an important-role checklist from the execution inventory. Include the primary subject, every required or narratively important role, every major object, and every important nested subject. No role may be skipped because another role of the same category passed.

Record one row per role with: activated modules, identity, style construction, visibility and scale, layer or interaction relation, and pass/fail. Any failed row blocks acceptance.

## Gates

### 1. First-read and rhythm

Pass when the intended primary subject remains first at thumbnail size, the largest quiet field stays continuous, and dense clusters remain separated by breathing gaps.

Fail when text, accents, background objects, or microdetail compete with the main silhouette, or when candidate cues fill protected space.

### 2. Role-style consistency

For every role on the important-role checklist, compare its universal card and activated category fields with the blueprint's decisive mechanisms:

- silhouette and proportion;
- line hierarchy;
- fill and value planes;
- lighting ceiling;
- material response;
- color adhesion;
- texture placement;
- detail density.

Also check required identity cues, visibility protection, effective frame share, interaction readability, and category-specific fields. For important environment roles, inspect spatial layers, landmark hierarchy, open-space protection, and atmospheric-depth ceiling.

Pass each role separately. The gate passes only when every important role belongs to the same construction system and remains legible at its intended scale. Matching palette and paper texture alone is insufficient, and a strong primary subject cannot hide a failed secondary role.

Common failures include anime facial anatomy inside a folk print, realistic portrait shading inside flat graphics, glossy 3D machinery inside symbolic collage, and detailed fur inside a low-detail shape system.

### 3. Inset integrity

Pass when the container remains legible, its effective full-frame share supports the chosen recognition cues, the primary action reads immediately, important identity and interaction cues remain visible, and the frame owns the closing contour.

Fail when the inset carries several equal actions, details bleed outside the boundary, or internal scenery overwhelms the figure.

### 4. Capacity and leakage

Pass when every secondary element has an assigned cluster, candidate pools were reduced to fit, and source-specific content has not leaked into the target.

Fail when all listed environment cues appear at equal salience, new unrequested labels or icons appear, or decoration spreads through a quiet field.

## Repair decision

Before a local edit, preserve the pre-edit output and write a frozen-invariant checklist covering composition, crop, primary silhouette, quiet fields, cluster boundaries, palette roles, text, every unaffected important role, and all unaffected inset boundaries.

Classify the dominant defect before editing:

- **Local role failure**: face, hand, figure, animal, prop, or one inset uses the wrong construction system. Edit only that region; freeze composition, palette, background, text, subject silhouette, and unaffected details.
- **Local identity failure**: a required emblem, antenna, valve, number, or prop is missing. Add only that cue; freeze all other content.
- **Inset overload**: remove subordinate candidate content or move one supported cue to an existing panel; keep the container and primary action.
- **Global density failure**: remove incidental content, reduce candidate cues, restore quiet fields, and regenerate once.
- **Global style failure**: restate the modeling, line, fill, light, and texture contract at the start; regenerate once with fewer style adjectives and fewer candidate objects.

Make one targeted correction per iteration. After the edit, inspect the full frame, thumbnail, target crop, every other important-role crop, and every frozen invariant against the pre-edit output. Accept the edit only when the target defect improves and no frozen invariant changes materially. Otherwise reject the edited output, retain the pre-edit output as the accepted version, and report the unresolved defect. Stop after the bounded correction rather than stacking uncontrolled revisions.
